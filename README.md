Here I have attached several documents I have written to explore ideas I find beautiful. Some are more formal and some are quite (!) informal. Some are incomplete but could still be interesting to interested readers.

In my experience, writing is an essential part of understanding. I find benefits both from writing tautly and writing with experimental curiosity. 


* **[Quant Finance & Information Theory](./ShannonKelly.pdf)**
  * This document highlights my journey to (re-re-re-...re-)discovery and appreciaton of the connection between quantitative finance and gambling and information theory. I initially had the idea that the **Implied Volatility** calculated by the **Black Scholes Option Pricing Model** was analogous to **Entropy** in information theory. I found that intuition to be slightly incorrect, but this led me to learn about the *true, and pure* equivalence between the wealth function maximized by the **Kelly Criterion** and the familiar **KL Divergence** value in information theory, statistics, and machine learning. Enjoy.
 
* **[Black Scholes as a Natural Transformation](./BlackScholesCT.pdf)**
  * In this document, I borrow intuition from Category Theory to represent the key constraint that defines derivative prices (and perhaps all asset pricing). I am trying to learn category theory, get my hands dirty with applying it. I admit freely that this is not a rigorous mapping of Black Scholes using CT, though it borrows CT's incredible representation of structure. There are many ways you could formulate the same point in CT. I tried many. This is a cool one that I landed on. 

* **[Why Overparameterization Works](./Why_Overparameterization_Works.pdf)**
  * Here, I chart my learning and understanding of why largely overparameterized machine learning functions can still achieve very good results without overfitting in the traditional sense. The standard wisdom that overly complex models will overfit has been shown to not hold up in certain scenarios. There has been observed a 'double descent' in the *testing* loss of machine learning models. In this document, I show how I came to understand why that is.

* **[Uncertainty in Fourier Transforms and Quantum Mechanics](./Fourier_Quantum_Uncertainty.pdf)**
  * Here, I step far out of my core competency and attempt to write self-teaching pedagogy for why quantum uncertainty of position and momentum occurs. I am very familiar with the *fundamental* time-frequency uncertainty in Fourier Transforms. I knew that there was a connection to quantum physics, and here I try to find it.

* **[On Computable Meanings](./On_Computable_Meanings.pdf)**
  * This is one of the first documents I wrote in the name of self-understanding. After reading ***Gödel, Escher, Bach*** and ***The Annotated Turing***, I felt the need to crystallize and store my understanding. These slippery concepts kept slipping! I wanted to walk and talk through my understanding, realize the diagrams and the in-depth examples that these fantastic books did not show, and create a document to capture some of the sparks that have sent me down the path of Computer Science and Mathematics.
  * It will never be 'finished'. Every new thing I learn, I want to impress into this document.

* **[Affinity](./Affinity.pdf)**
  * In this document, I casually discuss the ubiquity of *affinity* as an operator in complex computational systems. It is a self-contained, locally-computable operator that can compose powerfully.
 
* **[Attention Apprehension](./Attention_Apprehension.pdf)**
  * In this somewhat aggressive piece, I talk about how insane LLMs are. Specifically, I talk about how the intentional focal point could be the inputs, not the LLM itself. The attention mechanisms in the model are being entirely apprehended and shaped and snapped into place by the input, which becomes a lurched output.
 
* **[Generation](./Generation.pdf)**
  * Another casual and flexible piece that is the product of my learning about **Variational Autoencoders**. I am gesturing at the notion that data need not be stored in full; it can be compressed. If something can be generated on request, why do I care how it is stored? Does this apply to memory? Does it apply to quantum superposition?
